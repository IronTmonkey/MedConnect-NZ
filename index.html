Pathways

The routes to registration in New Zealand

The Medical Council of New Zealand (MCNZ) decides which route applies to you. These summaries explain the main routes so the questions make sense. Last checked October 2026.

Comparable health system pathway

Who it is for
Doctors with recent practice in one of 29 countries with a comparable health system.
Key requirements
At least 33 of the last 48 months practising, 20 or more hours a week, current full registration, and English requirements met.
Then
Verification of your degree through EPIC and a job offer that includes supervision.

NZREX Clinical route

Who it is for
Doctors who do not meet another pathway.
Key requirements
A prerequisite exam (PLAB Part 1, MCCQE Part I, AMC MCQ or USMLE Steps 1 and 2 CK) in the last 5 years, English requirements met, and degree verification.
Then
Pass NZREX Clinical, then supervised prevocational training in a New Zealand hospital (first and second postgraduate years).

General practice routes

Who it is for
Doctors who want to work as GPs.
Options
The GP training programme (GPEP), the Prior Specialist Training Pathway for recognised overseas GP qualifications (Group 1 or Group 2), and a GP fast-track for some GPs from the USA, Canada and Singapore.
Also ask about
The RNZCGP requirement for current advanced cardiac life support (ACLS) and the NZREX primary care pathway.

MCNZ also has a competent authority pathway and vocational scope routes for specialists. This tool covers the main general and GP routes.

Guides by country

Your route depends on where you practised recently, not only where you trained.

Doctors trained in China

China is not on the list of 29 comparable health system countries, so the usual route is NZREX Clinical. If you have practised recently in a country that is on the list, such as Singapore, Hong Kong or the UK, the comparable pathway may apply instead.

  • Check that your medical school and graduation year are covered in the World Directory of Medical Schools.
  • Pass a prerequisite exam, for example PLAB Part 1 or USMLE Steps 1 and 2 CK.
  • Take IELTS Academic or OET Medicine unless you qualify for an English exemption.
  • Start degree verification through EPIC early, because it takes time.

Doctors trained in India

India is not on the list of 29 comparable countries, so the usual route is NZREX Clinical. Recent practice in a listed country, with 33 of the last 48 months, may open the comparable pathway.

  • Check your medical school and graduation year in the World Directory of Medical Schools.
  • Pass a prerequisite exam, such as PLAB Part 1, MCCQE Part I, AMC MCQ or USMLE Steps 1 and 2 CK.
  • Take IELTS Academic or OET Medicine. Many Indian-trained doctors study in English, but the exemption needs the degree taught entirely in English in specific countries, so check the rule.
  • Start EPIC verification early.

Doctors trained or practising in the USA and Canada

The USA and Canada are both on the list of 29. With at least 33 of the last 48 months of recent practice, 20 or more hours a week and current full registration, the comparable pathway may apply without the NZREX Clinical exam.

  • GPs with an ABFM or CCFP qualification may be eligible for the GP fast-track. Confirm this with the RNZCGP.
  • English may be exempt if your degree was taught entirely in English in a listed country.
  • Ask the RNZCGP about the ACLS requirement.
  • Arrange a certificate of current professional standing from your state or provincial regulator.

Doctors trained in Europe, the UK and Ireland

Many European countries are on the list of 29, including the UK, Ireland, Germany, France, Spain, Italy and the Netherlands. Countries such as Poland, Romania and Hungary are not, so doctors trained there usually need NZREX Clinical unless they have practised recently in a listed country.

  • UK MRCGP or FRCGP and Irish MICGP are recognised GP qualifications in the RNZCGP lists.
  • English requirements still apply unless you qualify for an exemption.
  • Keep records of your hours and months of practice for the last four years.

Terms used on this site

MCNZ

The Medical Council of New Zealand. It decides who can register to practise medicine.

NZREX Clinical

The clinical exam MCNZ uses for doctors who do not qualify through another route.

EPIC and ECFMG

EPIC is the service that checks your qualifications directly with the school that issued them. It is run by ECFMG.

World Directory of Medical Schools (WDOMS)

A list of medical schools. MCNZ needs your school to be listed with an ECFMG Sponsor Note that covers your graduation year.

RNZCGP

The Royal New Zealand College of General Practitioners. It runs GP training and recognises GP qualifications.

Supervision

Working under a named senior doctor for a set period before you can practise independently.

About and help

About MedConnect NZ

MedConnect NZ helps overseas-trained doctors, and the practices that may employ them, understand which registration pathway is likely to apply. It explains the rules. It does not decide anything.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the official answer?

No. MCNZ decides registration eligibility. This tool shows the likely pathway and the tasks that follow from your answers.

What happens to my answers?

They stay in this browser tab. They are not sent to a server and they disappear when you close the tab. Save the PDF from your answer sheet to keep them.

Why does "Unknown" matter?

If you are not sure about an answer, the requirement is marked Unknown and a task is added to find out. Unknown is never treated as a pass.

Where does the list of 29 countries come from?

It is the list of comparable health system countries in the MCNZ registration policy. It is not the same as the number of medical schools in the World Directory.

How current is the information?

Rules were last checked in October 2026. Policies change, so confirm with MCNZ before you apply.

Can I go back and change an answer?

Yes. Use the Edit links beside your answers, or the Edit my answers button on the answer sheet. Questions that no longer apply are hidden.
